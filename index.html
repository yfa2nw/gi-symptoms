Esophagitis

Pathophysiology

Various causes lead to mucosal damage, which leads to the inflammation of the esophagus.

Etiology

Pill-induced

  • NSAIDs, KCl
  • Doxycycline
  • Bisphosphonates

Reflux-associated

  • GERD
  • Conditions that are nonresponsive to PPIs

Infectious

  • Immunocompromised patients
  • Candida infections Most common
  • CMV, HSV infections

Caustic

  • Ingestion of strong acid
  • Ingestion of strong base

Eosinophilic

Asthma Allergic rhinitis Atopic dermatitis
+
Food allergens

Symptoms and complications

Symptoms

  • Dysphagia
  • Odynophagia
  • Heartburn
  • Chest pain

Complications

  • Strictures and ulcers
  • Bleeding hematemesis, melena
  • Perforation pneumomediastinum, mediastinitis
  • Obstruction
  • Aspiration pneumonia
  • Barrett's esophagus Intestinal metaplasia
  • Esophageal adenocarcinoma Dysplasia

Diagnosis

If there is Obtain
Bleeding EGD with biopsy
Perforation Contrast esophagram and CXR, followed by surgery

Treatment

Always give PPIs for esophagitis to prevent further mucosal damage, then treat the cause.

Cause Treatment
Pill-induced Cease medications
Reflux-associated Treat
Infectious Prescribe antimicrobials
Caustic Avoid vomiting and go for surgery
Eosinophilic Avoid food allergens

Esophageal Motility Disorders

Pathophysiology

Neuromuscular dysfunctions of the esophagus cause dysphagia.

Etiology

Achalasia

  • Idiopathic
  • Trypanosoma cruzi
  • Decreased myenteric plexus activity
  • Decreased NO, VIP neurotransmitter activity
  • Increased LES tone
  • Decreased mid-distal esophageal motility

Diffuse esophageal spasm

  • Trigger
  • Disorganized mid-distal esophageal motility

Esophageal scleroderma

  • Atrophy
  • Fibrosis
  • Decreased LES tone
  • Decreased mid-distal esophageal motility

Symptoms and complications

Symptoms

  • Dysphagia

Complications

  • Aspiration pneumonia
  • Cancer

Diagnosis

Diagnostic tools include:

  • Barium swallow
  • EGD with biopsy
  • Manometry Gold standard

Treatment

Cause Treatment
Achalasia CCB or nitrates, pneumatic dilation, or botulinum injection
Diffuse esophageal spasm CCB or nitrates, pneumatic dilation, or botulinum injection
Esophageal scleroderma PPIs

Gastritis

Pathophysiology

Damage to the stomach leads to inflammation of the stomach's mucosal lining.

Associated with:

  • Antrum
  • Shallow erosions
  • Shallow mucosal layers

Etiology

NSAIDs and alcohol

  • COX inhibition
  • Decreased PG
  • Increased HCl production
  • Decreased mucus production

H. pylori

  • Infection
  • Increased gastrin production
  • Increased HCl production

Curling's ulcers

  • Severe burns
  • Hypovolemia
  • Mucosal ischemia

Cushing's ulcers

  • Increased ICP
  • Increased CN X activity
  • Increased submucosal activity
  • Increased HCl production

Autoimmune gastritis

  • Autoantibodies attack parietal cells
  • Decreased HCl and IF production
  • Fundal atrophy
  • Rather than inflammation or erosions

Note: Curling's and Cushing's ulcers are not true ulcers because they do not erode past the muscularis mucosae.

Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)

Pathophysiology

Damage to the stomach leads to ulceration of the mucosal lining, which extends beyond the muscularis mucosae.

Etiology

H. pylori Most common

Characteristics:

  • Gram-negative
  • Urease-positive
  • Multiple flagella
  • Lives in the gastric mucosa

Virulence factors:

  • Ureases
  • Cytotoxins (VacA, CagA)
  • Mucinases
  • Phospholipases
  • PAF

Chronic infection leads to:

  • Gastritis
  • Atrophy
  • Intestinal metaplasia
  • Dysplasia
  • Gastric cancer
  • Gastric MALT lymphoma

NSAIDs and alcohol 2nd most common

  • COX inhibition
  • Decreased PG
  • Increased HCl production
  • Decreased mucus production

Gastrinomas

  • Conditions such as Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
  • Gastrin production
  • Increased HCl production

Symptoms and complications

  • Epigastric

  • Vomiting
  • Heartburn

  • Perforation pneumoperitoneum, peritonitis

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

Pathophysiology

IBS is a type of DGBI that stems from visceral hypersensitivity, altered microbiome, and hypermotility.

Note: there is a lack of structural damage.

Etiology

Visceral hypersensitivity

  • Psychiatric disorders
    Fibromyalgia, depression, anxiety
  • Increased CNS amplification of stretch receptor stimulation from osmotic effects of carbohydrates
  • Abdominal pain

Altered microbiome

  • Gut microbiome metabolizes carbohydrates into gas
  • Stimulates stretch receptors
  • Reflex contraction
  • Abdominal pain

Hypermotility

  • Increased gut motility
  • Diarrhea

Symptoms and complications

Predominant bowel pattern:

  • Abdominal pain
  • Diarrhea
  • Constipation
  • Mixed

Diagnosis

Rome V criteria

Recurrent but not continuous Abdominal pain or discomfort for at least 3 days per month
And 2 of the following
  • Related to defecation
  • Changes in stool frequency
  • Changes in stool appearance
If there is Obtain
Perforation Abdominal X-ray and CT AP
Diarrhea Fecal calprotectin, ESR/CRP, O&P, C. diff, Colonoscopy with biopsy

Treatment

For Use
Diet Low FODMAP diet
Abdominal pain TCAs, Peppermint oil
Constipation Laxatives
Diarrhea Loperamide
